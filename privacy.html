Pulso Privacy Policy

Version 1.0 · effective November 2, 2026 · Español

This policy explains what information the iPhone app Pulso handles, where it is stored, who can see it and how to delete it. Pulso is a personal tool to keep track of your accounts, debts, receivables, expenses, income and net worth. The app is in Spanish; this is an English translation of the Spanish policy.

In short

  • What you record in Pulso is stored on your iPhone.
  • If you turn on backup, the app also saves copies in your own iCloud account (iCloud Drive, an Apple service), in a folder named Pulso that only you can open.
  • Pulso has no accounts of its own, no servers, analytics, ads or third-party tools, and it does not connect to your bank.
  • The developer does not receive and cannot see anything you record.
  • You decide when to share a report, a receipt or a backup, and with whom.
  • You can delete everything from the app itself or by uninstalling it.

1. Controller

The app is published on the App Store by Jefferson Huanca, Ecuador. Contact: jefferson.huanca@icloud.com.

2. Information the app handles

Only what you type, attach or choose to load:

  • Your finances: names and balances of your accounts, their currency and the exchange rates you enter; debts and credit cards (installments, dates, limits); money owed to you; transactions (date, type, amount, account, category, note); recurring payments, income and subscriptions; budgets, goals and month-end closings.
  • Your profile (optional): how the app should call you, your full name and ID number (they only appear on receipts you choose to send) and your date of birth (only for a birthday greeting).
  • Other people’s data (optional): the names of the people, businesses or institutions you have accounts with (who you lent money to, who you owe, your card issuer) and, if you enter them, their phone, ID and notes. They are stored only on your iPhone and in your backup; Pulso does not send them anywhere or use them for anything else. Enter only what you need and what the other person would expect you to note.
  • Receipt photos you choose to attach.
  • Files you load: an Excel template, a backup or your bank’s CSV transactions file. Pulso reads them on the phone, shows you what they contain and records only what you confirm. They are not sent anywhere.
  • Preferences: main currency, first day of the month, Face ID lock, reminders.

The app does not access your location, contacts, calendar, microphone, health data, advertising identifier, or usage or diagnostic data. It does not connect to banks or to any internet service.

3. Where it is stored

  • In the app’s private space on your iPhone, with iOS complete file protection: while the iPhone is locked with a passcode, not even the app can read it.
  • The “Próximos pagos” widget receives a summary of what is due (names, dates and amounts) on the same iPhone. When the iPhone is locked, amounts are hidden.
  • If you use iCloud Backup or back up your iPhone to a computer, iOS includes this data in that backup, under your own Apple Account.
  • If you turn on iCloud Drive backup, the app saves copies (ZIP files and photos) in the Pulso folder of your iCloud Drive. They use your iCloud storage and only your account can open them. When you close a month, the monthly report (PDF, Excel and CSV) is saved there too.
  • The app works offline and sends no information to any server of the developer or of third parties.

4. What the app does not do

It does not ask you to create an account or sign in. It has no servers. It includes no analytics, advertising, tracking or third-party SDKs. It does not sell, rent or share data. It does not build profiles or make automated decisions. It does not process payments or connect to your bank.

5. iPhone permissions

  • Photos: to attach images you already have; the iOS picker hands over only the ones you choose.
  • Camera: only when you tap “Tomar foto” to photograph a receipt; the photo is kept inside the app, not in your library.
  • Face ID: to open the app when the lock is on. The app receives no biometric data; iOS only tells it whether verification succeeded.
  • Notifications: due-date reminders are local notifications scheduled on your phone; they go through no server.

The app does not request location, contacts or microphone permissions.

6. When information leaves the phone

Only when you share a report (PDF, Excel, CSV, image), a receipt, an account statement, the template or a backup (ZIP) with the iOS share sheet, or when the app saves copies to your iCloud Drive if you turned it on. The backup file has no password: keep it somewhere safe. If you give the template to an artificial intelligence service, you do it yourself, with the service you choose, under that service’s policy.

7. Retention and deletion

Data stays until you delete it. Delete everything in the app: Más › Datos y respaldo › “Borrar todos los datos de este teléfono” (double confirmation). Uninstalling the app removes the data from the iPhone; it does not delete the iCloud Drive copies, which you can remove from Files › iCloud Drive › Pulso.

8. Security

Protection depends on your phone and your Apple Account: use a passcode and Face ID, keep iOS up to date and turn on two-factor authentication for your Apple Account.

9. Children

Pulso is a tool for adults. It is not directed to children under 13 and collects no data from anyone.

10. This website

Hosted on GitHub Pages. It uses no cookies, forms or analytics of its own. GitHub may log technical data about visits under its privacy statement.

11. Changes

If the app changes how it handles data, the new version is published on this page with its effective date and mentioned in the App Store update notes.

Pulso is a free iPhone app. No accounts, no servers, no ads.
